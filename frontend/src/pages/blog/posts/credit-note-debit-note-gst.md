Goods come back damaged. A client negotiates a discount after billing. You undercharged on an invoice and need to bill the difference. In all these cases you don't cancel or edit the original GST invoice. You issue a **credit note** or a **debit note**.

## Credit note vs debit note at a glance

| | Credit note | Debit note |
|---|---|---|
| Issued by | Supplier | Supplier (under GST) |
| Effect | **Reduces** taxable value or tax | **Increases** taxable value or tax |
| Common reasons | Goods returned, post-sale discount, excess charged, deficient service | Price increase, undercharged tax or value, extra quantity supplied |
| GST law | Section 34(1), CGST Act | Section 34(3), CGST Act |
| Supplier's tax liability | Goes down | Goes up |
| Buyer's ITC | Must be reduced | Can be increased |

Under GST, both are issued by the **supplier**. In everyday business, buyers sometimes send a "debit note" to the supplier when returning goods. That is a commercial document asking for a credit note, not a GST debit note.

## When to issue a credit note

Under Section 34(1), a registered supplier may issue a credit note when:

1. The taxable value or tax charged on the invoice is **more than** what's payable,
2. Goods supplied are **returned** by the buyer, or
3. Goods or services supplied are found to be **deficient**.

**Discounts after supply:** a post-supply discount can reduce the taxable value only if it was agreed in advance and can be linked to specific invoices, and if the buyer reverses the matching ITC. Otherwise, issue a "financial" or commercial credit note without any GST adjustment.

## When to issue a debit note

Under Section 34(3), a supplier issues a debit note when the taxable value or tax charged on the invoice was **less than** what's actually payable. For example:

- A price escalation clause raises the final price,
- The wrong (lower) GST rate was applied, or
- Extra quantity was supplied beyond the original invoice.

## Mandatory contents of a credit or debit note

Under Rule 53 of the CGST Rules, a credit or debit note should contain:

- The nature of the document: "Credit Note" or "Debit Note"
- Supplier's name, address and GSTIN
- A consecutive serial number (up to 16 characters, unique for the financial year)
- Date of issue
- Recipient's name, address and GSTIN (if registered)
- Serial number(s) and date(s) of the original tax invoice(s)
- Taxable value of goods/services, the GST rate, and the amount credited or debited
- Signature or digital signature of the supplier

Use a separate series for credit and debit notes, such as CN/26-27/001 and DN/26-27/001. The [invoice number generator](/tools/invoice-number-generator) can create matching formats.

## Time limit for credit notes

A credit note that reduces tax liability must be declared in a return no later than **30 November following the end of the financial year** in which the original supply was made, or the date of filing the annual return, **whichever is earlier**.

Example: for a supply invoiced in FY 2025-26, the credit note must be reported by 30 November 2026 at the latest. After that, you can still issue a commercial credit note, but you cannot reduce your GST liability.

Debit notes have no equivalent deadline for declaring them, because they increase tax. But the buyer's ability to claim ITC on them is subject to its own time limits, so issue them promptly.

## How they're reported in returns

- **Supplier:** report credit and debit notes in **GSTR-1** (in the B2B or B2C credit/debit note sections, as applicable). The net effect flows into the tax liability in **GSTR-3B**.
- **Buyer:** notes reported by the supplier appear in the buyer's **GSTR-2B**. The buyer reduces ITC for credit notes and can claim additional ITC for debit notes.
- **E-invoicing:** if you're required to generate e-invoices, credit and debit notes must also be reported to the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP) to get an IRN. See our guide to [e-invoicing under GST](/blog/e-invoicing-under-gst-requirements).

## Worked examples

### Example 1: goods returned

Mehta Textiles (Gujarat) sold 100 shirts at ₹500 each to a Maharashtra retailer: taxable value ₹50,000 with 5% IGST (₹2,500). Ten shirts are returned.

- Credit note value: 10 × ₹500 = ₹5,000
- IGST reversed: 5% of ₹5,000 = ₹250
- Total credit: **₹5,250**, referencing the original invoice number and date

### Example 2: price increase (debit note)

A contractor billed ₹2,00,000 + 18% GST (₹36,000). Under the price escalation clause, the final price rises by ₹20,000.

- Debit note taxable value: ₹20,000
- GST at 18%: ₹3,600 (CGST ₹1,800 + SGST ₹1,800 if intra-state)
- Total debit: **₹23,600**

## Common mistakes

1. **Editing or cancelling the original invoice** after it's reported, instead of issuing a note.
2. **Missing the original invoice reference** on the note.
3. **Missing the 30 November deadline** for credit notes.
4. **Reducing GST on discounts not agreed in advance.**
5. **Buyers not reversing ITC** for credit notes received, which can lead to mismatches and notices.
6. **Using the invoice series** for credit/debit notes instead of a separate series.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a credit note under GST?

A credit note is a document issued by a registered supplier to reduce the taxable value or tax on an earlier invoice, for example when goods are returned, the price was overcharged, or services were deficient. It is governed by Section 34(1) of the CGST Act.

### What is the time limit to issue a credit note under GST?

It must be declared in a return by 30 November following the end of the financial year of the original supply, or by the date of filing the annual return, whichever is earlier.

### Who issues a debit note under GST?

Under GST, the supplier issues a debit note when the value or tax on the original invoice was lower than it should have been. A debit note sent by a buyer for a purchase return is a commercial document, not a GST debit note.

### Can one credit note cover multiple invoices?

Yes. A single credit or debit note can be issued against multiple invoices issued to the same recipient, as long as the details of the original invoices are referenced.

### Do credit notes need an IRN under e-invoicing?

Yes. Businesses that must generate e-invoices also need to report credit and debit notes to the Invoice Registration Portal and obtain an IRN for them.
