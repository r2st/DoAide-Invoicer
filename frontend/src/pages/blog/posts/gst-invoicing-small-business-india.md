If you run a small business in India — a shop, a service firm, a freelance practice — you have probably heard that GST invoicing is mandatory above a certain turnover. But what exactly must go on the invoice? Which type do you issue? And what happens if you get it wrong?

This guide covers all the essentials of **GST invoicing for small businesses** in 2026. It is general information, not tax advice — confirm the details with a chartered accountant or on the [GST Portal](https://www.gst.gov.in).

## Who needs to issue GST invoices?

GST registration is generally required once your aggregate turnover in a financial year crosses:

- **₹40 lakh** for goods suppliers in most states (₹20 lakh in special category states like the north-eastern states, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, etc.)
- **₹20 lakh** for service providers (₹10 lakh in special category states)

Once registered, every sale of goods or services must be accompanied by a **tax invoice** that meets the format prescribed in Rule 46 of the CGST Rules, 2017.

Businesses below the threshold can still register voluntarily — and often should, if their clients are GST-registered businesses who want to claim Input Tax Credit (ITC).

## Mandatory fields on a GST tax invoice (Rule 46)

A valid GST tax invoice must include all of the following:

1. **Supplier name, address and GSTIN** — your registered business details.
2. **A consecutive serial number** — unique for the financial year, containing only alphabets, numerals or special characters like dash or slash. For example: `INV-2026-001`.
3. **Date of issue.**
4. **Recipient name and address** — and their GSTIN, if registered.
5. **HSN code** (for goods) or **SAC code** (for services) — the number of digits depends on your turnover (see below).
6. **Description of goods or services.**
7. **Quantity and unit** (for goods).
8. **Taxable value** — the value before tax, after any discounts.
9. **Tax rate and tax amount** — split into CGST + SGST (intra-state) or IGST (inter-state).
10. **Total invoice value** — taxable value plus tax.
11. **Place of supply** — determines whether the transaction is intra-state or inter-state.
12. **Whether tax is payable on reverse charge.**
13. **Signature or digital signature** of the supplier or an authorised representative.

Missing even one of these can make the invoice invalid for ITC claims by your client — which means they may push back and ask for a corrected invoice.

## Types of GST invoices

Not every sale needs the same document:

### Tax invoice
The standard invoice for taxable supplies. This is what most businesses issue day-to-day. It must contain all the fields listed above.

### Bill of supply
Issued instead of a tax invoice when:
- You are a **composition scheme** dealer, or
- You are supplying **exempt goods or services**.

A bill of supply does not show any tax amount because no tax is being charged. It still needs the supplier and recipient details, description and value.

### Credit note
Issued when you need to reduce the value of an earlier invoice — for example, when goods are returned, a discount is given after the sale, or tax was charged at a higher rate than applicable.

### Debit note
Issued when the value needs to increase — for example, when tax was initially charged at a lower rate.

For more on credit and debit notes, see our [detailed guide on credit notes and debit notes under GST](/blog/credit-note-debit-note-gst).

## HSN and SAC codes — when are they required?

The number of HSN/SAC digits you must mention on invoices depends on your turnover:

| Annual Turnover | HSN/SAC Digits Required |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹5 crore | 4 digits |
| Above ₹5 crore | 6 digits |

Since April 2021, mentioning HSN/SAC codes on invoices is mandatory for all GST-registered businesses, regardless of turnover. Getting the code right matters — it determines the applicable tax rate.

## CGST + SGST vs IGST — when does each apply?

This is one of the most common sources of confusion:

- **Intra-state supply** (seller and buyer are in the same state): charge **CGST + SGST**. Each is half the total GST rate. For example, at 18% GST, you charge 9% CGST and 9% SGST.
- **Inter-state supply** (seller and buyer are in different states): charge **IGST** at the full rate. At 18% GST, you charge 18% IGST.
- **Exports and supplies to SEZs**: these are treated as inter-state and charged IGST, though they are zero-rated (you can supply under LUT without charging, or charge and claim a refund).

The **place of supply** rules (Sections 10–13 of the IGST Act) determine whether a transaction is intra-state or inter-state. For goods, it is generally where the goods are delivered. For services, it depends on the type of service and the location of the supplier and recipient.

## Filing deadlines and late fees

GST-registered businesses must file periodic returns that include invoice details:

### GSTR-1 (Outward supplies)
- **Monthly filers** (turnover above ₹5 crore, or opted for monthly): due by the **11th of the following month**.
- **Quarterly filers** (QRMP scheme, turnover up to ₹5 crore): due by the **13th of the month following the quarter**.

### GSTR-3B (Summary return with tax payment)
- **Monthly**: due by the **20th of the following month**.
- **Quarterly** (QRMP scheme): due by the **22nd or 24th of the month following the quarter**, depending on the state.

### Late fee for delayed filing
- **GSTR-1**: ₹50 per day of delay (₹25 CGST + ₹25 SGST), capped at ₹10,000 per return. Nil returns attract ₹20 per day.
- **GSTR-3B**: same structure — ₹50 per day, capped. Plus **interest at 18% per annum** on the tax liability paid late.

Filing on time every month is far cheaper than paying penalties later. Keeping your invoices organised and correctly formatted from day one makes filing straightforward.

## Common GST invoicing mistakes small businesses make

1. **Wrong HSN/SAC code** — leads to wrong tax rate and potential notices during assessment.
2. **Missing GSTIN of the recipient** — your client cannot claim ITC without their GSTIN on the invoice.
3. **Not splitting CGST/SGST vs IGST correctly** — charging IGST on an intra-state sale (or vice versa) is a compliance error.
4. **Issuing a tax invoice under the composition scheme** — composition dealers must issue a bill of supply, never a tax invoice.
5. **Non-sequential invoice numbers** — the GST rules require consecutive serial numbers. Gaps invite scrutiny.
6. **Forgetting the place of supply** — especially relevant for service businesses operating across state borders.
7. **Not issuing credit/debit notes** — adjustments to earlier invoices must be documented with the proper note, not just a revised invoice.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is GST registration mandatory for all small businesses?

No. GST registration is required only when your aggregate turnover crosses the threshold — ₹40 lakh for goods suppliers or ₹20 lakh for service providers in most states. Below the threshold, registration is voluntary. However, businesses that supply inter-state or sell through e-commerce platforms may need to register regardless of turnover.

### Can I issue a GST invoice without an HSN code?

No. Since April 2021, mentioning HSN/SAC codes on invoices is mandatory for all GST-registered businesses. You need at least 4 digits if your turnover is up to ₹5 crore, and 6 digits if it is above ₹5 crore. Use the [HSN lookup tool](/hsn) to find the right code.

### What is the penalty for issuing an incorrect GST invoice?

Under Section 122 of the CGST Act, issuing an incorrect invoice can attract a penalty of ₹10,000 or the tax amount involved, whichever is higher. Additionally, your recipient may lose their ITC claim, which can damage the business relationship.

### Do I need to mention both CGST and SGST separately on the invoice?

Yes, for intra-state supplies. The tax must be split into equal parts of CGST and SGST. For inter-state supplies, you mention IGST as a single amount. The split is not optional — it is required by the invoice rules.

### Can I use a handwritten invoice for GST?

Technically, the law does not prohibit handwritten invoices as long as they contain all mandatory fields. However, handwritten invoices are error-prone, hard to organise for return filing, and give an unprofessional impression. A digital invoice from a tool like the [free invoice generator](/create) is faster, accurate and looks better.

### What is the difference between a tax invoice and a bill of supply?

A tax invoice is issued for taxable supplies and includes the tax amount. A bill of supply is issued when no tax is charged — either because the goods or services are exempt, or because the supplier is registered under the composition scheme. Both require supplier and recipient details, but only the tax invoice shows the tax breakup.
