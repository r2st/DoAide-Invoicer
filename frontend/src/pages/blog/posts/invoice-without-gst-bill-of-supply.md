Not every business in India charges GST. Lakhs of small shops, home businesses, tutors and service providers operate below the GST threshold. Others are registered under the composition scheme, or sell goods that are exempt from GST. All of them still need to give customers a proper bill.

So what does an **invoice without GST** look like, and when do you need a **bill of supply** instead? This guide explains the options, with formats, and clears up the common confusion between a *kacha bill* and a *pakka bill*. You can make any of these with the [free invoice generator](/create).

## Three kinds of bills in India

| Document | Who issues it | GST charged? | Buyer can claim ITC? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple invoice / bill | Businesses **not registered** under GST | No | No |
| Bill of supply | Registered businesses selling **exempt** goods/services, or **composition** taxpayers | No | No |
| Tax invoice | Regular GST-registered businesses making taxable supplies | Yes | Yes (if conditions met) |

The key rule is simple: **only a regular GST-registered person can collect GST** and issue a tax invoice. If you're unregistered or under composition, you must not show GST on your bill.

## 1. Invoice without GST (unregistered businesses)

Businesses below the registration threshold, generally ₹40 lakh for goods suppliers in most states (₹20 lakh in some) and ₹20 lakh for service providers (₹10 lakh in special category states), can choose not to register. Their bill should include:

- Business name, address and phone number
- Customer name (and address for larger amounts)
- Bill number and date
- Description, quantity, rate and amount for each item
- Total amount
- Payment mode (cash, UPI, bank transfer)

**Don't** write "Tax Invoice", show GSTIN fields or add any tax line. Saying "Not registered under GST" on the bill helps avoid confusion.

## 2. Bill of supply format

A **bill of supply** is issued by a registered person instead of a tax invoice when:

- they sell **exempt** goods or services (for example, fresh vegetables, unbranded food grains or certain educational services), or
- they are registered under the **composition scheme**.

Under the GST rules (Rule 49), a bill of supply should contain:

1. Supplier's name, address and GSTIN
2. A consecutive serial number (up to 16 characters, unique for the financial year)
3. Date of issue
4. Recipient's name, address and GSTIN (if registered)
5. HSN code of goods or SAC of services (as applicable)
6. Description of goods or services
7. Value of supply after discounts
8. Signature or digital signature

**Composition dealers** must also print this declaration at the top: *"composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies"*.

## 3. When you must issue a tax invoice instead

If you're a regular GST-registered business making taxable supplies, you need a full tax invoice with GSTIN, HSN/SAC, taxable value, the rate and the CGST/SGST or IGST amounts. See our [GST invoice format guide](/blog/gst-invoice-format-2026-complete-guide) and check your invoice with the [invoice validator](/tools/invoice-validator).

If a single sale includes both taxable and exempt items, a registered business can usually issue one "invoice-cum-bill of supply" covering both.

## Kacha bill vs pakka bill

You'll often hear shopkeepers ask, *"kacha bill chahiye ya pakka?"* (do you want an informal bill or a proper one?)

- **Pakka bill:** a proper invoice: a GST tax invoice from a registered seller, or a valid bill of supply. It's recorded in the seller's books and returns, and it is valid for warranty, reimbursement and ITC (where applicable).
- **Kacha bill:** an informal estimate or rough slip, often on plain paper, which may not be recorded in the books.

As a buyer, always ask for a pakka bill for anything you may need to return, claim warranty on, get reimbursed for, or claim ITC on. As a seller, issuing kacha bills to hide sales is tax evasion and can attract heavy penalties. A proper bill also builds customer trust.

## Do small shops need to issue bills?

Registered businesses must issue a tax invoice or bill of supply as the law requires. For small retail sales to unregistered customers, the rules allow a consolidated invoice at the end of the day for low-value sales below a specified limit, unless the customer asks for a bill. Whether registered or not, giving customers a simple bill is good practice. It helps with record-keeping, income-tax filing and loan applications.

## Common mistakes

1. **Charging GST without registration.** This is illegal, and the money collected must be paid to the government along with penalties.
2. **Composition dealers showing tax** on their bills.
3. **Missing serial numbers** or duplicate numbers. Use the [invoice number generator](/tools/invoice-number-generator).
4. **Calling a non-GST bill a "Tax Invoice".**
5. **Not keeping copies.** Every bill should be recorded for income-tax purposes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I make a bill without GST in India?

Yes. If you are not registered under GST, you can issue a simple bill without any GST. Registered businesses selling exempt goods or services, and composition taxpayers, issue a bill of supply without charging GST.

### What is a bill of supply under GST?

A bill of supply is a document issued by a registered person instead of a tax invoice when they supply exempt goods or services or are registered under the composition scheme. It contains the supplier and buyer details, serial number, date, description and value, but no tax amount.

### What is the difference between a kacha bill and a pakka bill?

A pakka bill is a proper invoice, either a GST tax invoice or a valid bill of supply, that is recorded in the seller's books. A kacha bill is an informal estimate or slip that may not be recorded. Always ask for a pakka bill for warranty, reimbursement or tax purposes.

### Can a composition dealer issue a tax invoice?

No. Composition taxpayers cannot collect GST from customers, so they issue a bill of supply with the declaration that they are a composition taxable person not eligible to collect tax on supplies.

### Can a buyer claim input tax credit on a bill of supply?

No. Because no GST is charged on a bill of supply, there is no input tax credit available to the buyer.
