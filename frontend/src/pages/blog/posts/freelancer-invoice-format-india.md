## Do freelancers need GST registration?

For service providers, GST registration is generally required once aggregate turnover in a financial year crosses **₹20 lakh** (₹10 lakh in certain special category states). Below that, registration is usually optional.

Some practical points:

- **Below the threshold, you can still register voluntarily.** This makes sense if most of your clients are GST-registered businesses who can claim input tax credit.
- **Export of services:** freelancers billing foreign clients often fall under relief for inter-state service suppliers below the threshold. If you do register, exports are "zero-rated" and you can supply under a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) without charging IGST.
- **Online platforms:** rules differ for some e-commerce and online information services. Check your specific case.

## Freelancer invoice format (without GST)

If you're not registered under GST, issue a simple invoice. Don't charge GST and don't call it a "tax invoice".

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Title | "Invoice" |
| Your details | Name, address, phone, email, PAN |
| Client details | Name, company, address |
| Invoice number | FL-2026-27/014 (unique and sequential) |
| Invoice date and due date | 22 Oct 2026, due 6 Nov 2026 |
| Description of services | "Logo design – 3 concepts, 2 revisions" |
| Amount | ₹25,000 |
| Payment details | Bank account, IFSC, UPI ID |
| Note | "Not registered under GST" (optional but helpful) |

## Freelancer invoice format (with GST)

Once registered, you must issue a proper **tax invoice** with the mandatory GST fields:

- Your GSTIN, and the client's GSTIN if they are registered
- A unique invoice number (up to 16 characters) and the date
- Place of supply (the client's state for most B2B services)
- SAC code for your service
- Taxable value, GST rate, and the tax split: **CGST + SGST** for intra-state, **IGST** for inter-state
- Total invoice value in figures and words
- Signature (physical or digital)

**Example:** A Pune-based developer billing a Bengaluru company ₹1,00,000 at 18% charges IGST of ₹18,000, because the supply is inter-state. Billing a Mumbai company instead would mean CGST ₹9,000 + SGST ₹9,000.

## Invoicing foreign clients: export of services

Billing clients in the US, UK, Europe or the Middle East is common for Indian freelancers. For a supply to count as an **export of services** under GST, broadly:

1. You (the supplier) are in India,
2. The client (the recipient) is outside India,
3. The place of supply is outside India,
4. Payment is received in convertible foreign exchange (or in INR where RBI permits), and
5. You and the client are not merely establishments of the same person.

### Export invoice format

If you're GST-registered and exporting under LUT:

- Title: "Tax Invoice" (or "Export Invoice")
- Client's name and full foreign address. No GSTIN is needed.
- Currency: USD/GBP/EUR, with the INR equivalent if you like
- Endorsement: **"Supply meant for export under Letter of Undertaking without payment of IGST"**
- LUT reference number
- IGST: 0 (under LUT)

File your LUT online on the GST portal (Form GST RFD-11) at the start of each financial year. If you're **not** registered, issue a normal invoice in the foreign currency without any GST.

### Getting paid from abroad

Payments usually arrive through bank wire, PayPal, Wise, Payoneer or similar platforms. Keep the foreign inward remittance proof (FIRC or the bank's advice) for each payment. It is useful for GST records, income tax and any refund claims.

## TDS on freelancer payments

Indian business clients often deduct **TDS** from professional or technical fees, for example under Section 194J, when payments cross the specified thresholds. So:

- Mention the gross amount on the invoice; the client deducts TDS when paying.
- Check that the TDS appears against your PAN in Form 26AS / AIS.
- Claim credit for it when you file your income-tax return.

Many freelancers also use the presumptive taxation scheme for professionals (Section 44ADA). Ask your CA whether it suits you.

## Payment terms that get freelancers paid faster

- **Ask for an advance:** 30-50% upfront for new clients is normal.
- **Use short terms:** "Due on receipt" or Net 7/Net 15 rather than Net 30.
- **Spell out late fees** in your contract and on the invoice.
- **Add a UPI QR code or payment link** for Indian clients.
- **Send reminders politely and on schedule.**

## Common freelancer invoicing mistakes

1. Charging GST without being registered. This is not allowed.
2. Reusing or skipping invoice numbers.
3. Vague descriptions like "Work done". Be specific, so the client's accounts team approves it quickly.
4. Missing bank or UPI details.
5. Wrong tax type (CGST/SGST instead of IGST for an out-of-state client).
6. Forgetting the LUT endorsement on export invoices.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a freelancer issue an invoice without GST in India?

Yes. If you are not registered under GST, typically because your turnover is below the threshold, you can issue a simple invoice without charging GST. Do not call it a tax invoice or show any GST amount.

### What is the GST registration limit for freelancers?

For service providers, registration is generally required when aggregate turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh in a financial year, or ₹10 lakh in certain special category states. You can register voluntarily below this limit.

### Do I need to charge GST to foreign clients?

Export of services is zero-rated. If you are GST-registered, you can export under a Letter of Undertaking without charging IGST, provided the conditions for export of services are met. If you are not registered, you simply invoice without GST.

### What should a freelancer invoice include?

Your name, contact details and PAN; the client's details; a unique invoice number; invoice and due dates; a clear description of services; the amount; payment details; and, if registered, your GSTIN, SAC code, place of supply and tax breakdown.

### Which invoice number format should freelancers use?

Use a simple, unique, sequential format that resets or includes the financial year, such as FL-2026-27/001. GST rules allow up to 16 characters for registered businesses.
